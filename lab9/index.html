Lab 9: Geospatial Visualization with D3

This page presents two coordinated visualizations of 2025 nominal GDP: a choropleth map and a cartogram.

Dataset Description

Data source: IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025
Variable: 2025 nominal GDP (billions of USD)
Countries shown: Top 50 economies
Geographic boundaries: Natural Earth (via DataHub.io)
Projection: Natural Earth 1
Color scale: Log-scaled blue sequential (due to GDP skew)

Choropleth Map

Color represents 2025 GDP. Geographic area is preserved.

Cartogram

Circle area represents 2025 GDP.

Design and Comparison

The choropleth preserves true geographic shapes and positions, making it easier to locate countries and recognize familiar borders. GDP is encoded through a logarithmic blue color scale because the raw values are extremely skewed, and a linear scale would make most countries appear nearly identical. Countries without GDP data are shown in light grey as missing, not zero.

The cartogram uses the same GDP values but encodes them by area instead of color. Each country is represented by a circle whose area is proportional to GDP. Large economies such as the United States, China, and Germany appear visually dominant, while small economies shrink. The trade-off is that geographic shapes are replaced by circles, but global economic magnitude becomes immediately visible.

Linked highlighting connects the two views: hovering over a country in either map highlights the same country in the other, helping users maintain correspondence between the two representations.

Task suitability: The choropleth is best for geographic questions (where is a country, what are regional patterns). The cartogram is best for magnitude questions (who has the largest economy, how do sizes compare). Neither is universally better — the choice depends on the analytical task.
